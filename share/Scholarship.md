### Comprehensive Master's & PhD Scholarship Search

2026–2027 admissions planning · Computer Science, AI, ML, Deep Learning, Data Science & Bioinformatics

The best way to approach this is to build a broad, ranked university database, rather than just a list of famous universities. We should include fully funded PhD programs, funded research master's programs, scholarships covering part of tuition, and government-funded international scholarships.

I'll organize the search around six things that matter most:

* University demand and reputation — research strength, global standing, and competitiveness in your target fields.

* Funding — tuition waiver, stipend, living allowance, health insurance, travel grants, or partial tuition support.

* Language and admission tests — IELTS, TOEFL, PTE, GRE, or alternatives.

* Minimum GPA — official minimum where published, plus realistic competitiveness.

* Work experience — whether it is required, optional, or irrelevant to the standard academic route.

* Other qualifications — degree prerequisites, research experience, publications, recommendation letters, portfolios, and application deadlines.

One important distinction: a university offering scholarships does not mean every admitted student receives one. PhD funding is often attached to a specific research position or admission offer, while master's funding is frequently more competitive and less comprehensive.

### 7\. Admission requirements: GPA, IELTS, TOEFL, PTE, GRE and work experience

This is the most important section for turning the university list into an actionable shortlist.

### A. English-language requirements

The following are practical planning ranges, not universal admission minimums. Each university and degree sets its own rules.

| Test                             | Typical planning target                                                   | Important note                                                                     |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| IELTS Academic                   | 6.5–7.5                                                                   | Some programs require minimum component scores                                     |
| TOEFL iBT                        | 90–105+                                                                   | Requirements and scoring scales can change                                         |
| PTE Academic                     | 60–75+                                                                    | Not accepted by every graduate program                                             |
| GRE General Test                 | Optional at many universities; competitive scores may help where accepted | Some programs do not accept or require it                                          |
| Duolingo English Test            | 120–135+                                                                  | Acceptance varies considerably                                                     |
| University English-medium waiver | Depends on university                                                     | Previous English-medium education may qualify in some cases                        |
| Country-specific language        | Depends on country/program                                                | German, French, Japanese, Korean, Chinese or other language requirements may apply |

For example, UBC's CS MSc program lists IELTS Academic 7.0, TOEFL iBT 100 on the pre-January 2026 scale, and no GRE requirement. Always check the currently applicable test scale and the program's own page.

Important: PTE acceptance is not guaranteed just because a university accepts IELTS and TOEFL. Verify the exact test list for each program before paying for an examination.

### B. GPA requirements

There is no universal GPA minimum for CS, AI, ML or bioinformatics. Some programs state a formal cutoff, while others assess the entire academic record.

| GPA on a 4.0 scale | Suggested application strategy                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 3.8–4.0            | Apply to highly competitive research universities, plus funded alternatives                                                                                    |
| 3.5–3.79           | Strong mix of ambitious and realistic research-focused programs                                                                                                |
| 3.2–3.49           | Broaden the list; emphasize research experience, recommendations and supervisor fit                                                                            |
| 3.0–3.19           | Prioritize programs whose published minimum you meet and strengthen evidence of research ability                                                               |
| Below 3.0          | Investigate alternative eligibility routes, relevant master's programs, grading-equivalence policies and programs that explicitly consider nonstandard records |

These are planning bands, not official admission cutoffs or guarantees. Your university's grading system, class rank, course difficulty, and grades in mathematics and CS subjects can materially affect the assessment.

Government scholarships may impose separate academic thresholds. For example, GKS generally uses an 80% or equivalent qualifying academic threshold, while Türkiye Scholarships publishes a 75% graduate academic-achievement threshold.

| Program type                           | Is employment experience required? | What matters most                                                 |

| PhD in CS/ML                           | Usually not as formal employment   | Research ability, recommendations, supervisor fit                 |

A publication is not always mandatory for PhD admission. However, a well-executed thesis, research internship, reproducible project, strong recommendation from a research supervisor, or credible preprint can help demonstrate readiness for research.

### 8\. What does “fully funded” actually cover?

Always evaluate the net funding package, not just the scholarship headline.

| Funding component | What to verify                                                                      |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tuition           | Full international tuition or only a partial waiver?                                |
| Stipend           | Monthly or annual amount, and number of funded years                                |
| Living costs      | Is the stipend sufficient for rent, food and transport in that city?                |
| Health insurance  | Fully covered, partly covered or paid separately?                                   |
| Student fees      | Mandatory fees covered or excluded?                                                 |
| Travel            | Airfare, visa, relocation or conference travel included?                            |
| Research costs    | Laptop, software, fieldwork, laboratory and conference support                      |
| Funding duration  | Does funding cover the entire degree?                                               |
| Conditions        | GPA maintenance, teaching duties, satisfactory progress and supervisor availability |

For example, UBC's published CS funding package separates its total support from tuition and student fees. ETH Zurich and EPFL generally recruit doctoral candidates through paid research positions, so the salary is not equivalent to a scholarship's gross value.

### 9\. Recommended application strategy for 2027 entry

As of October 25, 2026, some 2027-entry scholarship and graduate deadlines are approaching. The Cambridge Computer Science funding page lists December 8, 2026, as a key funding deadline for eligible applicants; Oxford's published Oxford–UBS applied-AI DPhil scholarships list December 2, 2026. Check each current call before submitting.

### Your application roadmap

* October–November 2026: Identify programs, research supervisors and open PhD vacancies. Prepare transcripts, CV, passport, references and English-test evidence.

* November 2026–January 2027: Submit suitable US PhD, UK doctoral, European research-position and Erasmus Mundus applications. Many deadlines fall within this period, but not all do.

* December 2026–March 2027: Apply to eligible university and government scholarship calls, including relevant European and Asian opportunities as they open.

* Throughout the cycle: Monitor university vacancies, supervisor funding, scholarship updates and alternative intakes. Some European doctoral positions recruit throughout the year.

### Documents to prepare

* Academic CV, normally 1–2 pages for master's applications and up to 2–3 pages for research-heavy PhD applications.

* Official transcripts and degree certificates.

* IELTS, TOEFL or another accepted language score, if required.

* Statement of purpose or research statement.

* Two or three recommendation letters, as specified by each university.

* Research proposal, where required.

* GitHub portfolio, thesis, research papers or relevant technical projects.

* Passport and scholarship-specific financial or eligibility documents.
