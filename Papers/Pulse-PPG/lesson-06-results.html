Lesson 6 — Experimental Results and Critical Interpretation

What the reported comparisons show—and what they do not establish

← Previous lessonContentsNext →

1. Main comparison with PPG models

The paper reports Pulse-PPG outperforming PaPaGei on 10 of 11 downstream tasks. The reported exception is sleep disturbance, where the paper discusses the importance of sleep-related pretraining data that was not represented in the same way in MOODS. Field pretraining is therefore not universally superior for every task; the match between pretraining data and downstream task matters.

2. General time-series foundation models

The paper compares Pulse-PPG with Chronos and MOMENT, alongside conventional baselines such as ResNet and random forest.

3. Field versus clinical pretraining

The paper reports Pulse-PPG pretrained on field data outperforming its clinical-pretraining counterpart on 10 of 11 tasks. The reported MOODS stress F1 difference is +0.0848 and activity F1 difference is +0.1218. This supports the value of realistic field data for several evaluated tasks, but does not isolate every dataset or objective confound.

4. Example downstream results

  • WESAD binary stress: F1 0.8759 with LP and 0.9392 with FT.
  • MOODS activity: F1 0.6859 with LP and 0.7992 with FT.
  • PPG-DaLiA instantaneous heart-rate MAE: 8.936 with LP and 3.705 with FT.

These are task-specific values and should not be compared across different tasks as if they shared a common scale.

5. Model size and input duration

The paper describes a light Pulse-PPG variant with approximately 4.73M parameters, compared with PaPaGei-S at approximately 5.7M. It reports four-minute windows performing best overall relative to one- and two-minute alternatives, and normalization improving performance in the evaluated setup.

6. What the results support

  • Field-trained representations can transfer effectively to several laboratory and field downstream tasks.
  • Pretraining data distribution matters; field data is not automatically best for every target.
  • Fine-tuning can provide substantial task-specific gains.
  • A smaller model can remain competitive in the reported comparisons.

7. What the results do not prove

  • They do not establish clinical diagnostic validity for every downstream task.
  • A blood-pressure-related regression result is not equivalent to validated clinical blood-pressure measurement.
  • Performance on finite datasets does not guarantee robustness across populations, devices, or environments.
  • High segment counts do not remove the need for subject-independent evaluation.
  • Aggregate metrics can conceal task-specific failures and subgroup disparities.
Critical reading exercise

Choose one reported improvement. List three alternative explanations besides “the representation is intrinsically better,” such as pretraining-data overlap, label quality, split differences, fine-tuning budget, or normalization.
