Lesson 2 — Related Work and Positioning

Where Pulse-PPG fits among supervised, self-supervised, and time-series foundation models

← Previous lessonContentsNext →

1. Main families of PPG learning

Supervised PPG models

These learn from labeled examples, such as PPG windows paired with heart rate, stress, activity, or blood-pressure labels. They can be effective when labels are abundant and reliable, but labels are expensive, task-specific, and sometimes noisy.

Self-supervised learning

Self-supervised methods create training signals from the data itself: masked reconstruction, future-sample prediction, contrastive views, or temporal-order prediction. They exploit unlabeled wearable data, but the pretext task must encourage physiologically useful features.

PPG foundation models

These seek reusable PPG representations trained at scale. Pulse-PPG is positioned alongside work such as PaPaGei and PPG-PT, emphasizing large-scale field pretraining.

General time-series foundation models

Chronos and MOMENT target broader time-series settings. They are useful comparison points, but general time-series models and sensor-specific PPG models may have different inductive biases, data, and objectives.

2. Why ordinary contrastive learning is not straightforward for PPG

Contrastive learning typically pulls together two views of the same underlying example and pushes apart unrelated examples. Image augmentations do not automatically transfer to physiological waveforms.

  • Amplitude scaling can alter physiologically meaningful amplitude information.
  • Time warping may change pulse timing or morphology.
  • Shuffling destroys temporal structure.
  • Noise can mimic sensor corruption, but excessive noise may erase useful morphology.

An augmentation that encourages invariance may also remove information needed by a downstream physiological task.

3. Pulse-PPG's distinction

Rather than defining similarity only through generic augmentations, Pulse-PPG first learns a motif-based distance, then uses relative contrastive learning to organize representations according to that distance.

5. Possible research directions

  • Disentangle physiology from motion and sensor artifacts.
  • Fuse PPG with accelerometer data.
  • Introduce temporal-order-aware motif matching.
  • Improve cross-device and cross-study generalization.
  • Estimate uncertainty and signal quality.
  • Optimize representations for edge deployment.
Research exercise
