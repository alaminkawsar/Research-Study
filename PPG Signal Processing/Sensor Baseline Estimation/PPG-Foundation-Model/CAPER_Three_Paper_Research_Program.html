The research program at a glance

Three linked papers, but each must answer a separate research question. The first is designed for public-data feasibility; the third is conditional on collecting appropriately validated clinical measurements.

PAPER 01 · FIRST PRIORITY

CAPER Baselines

Learn a person's expected PPG embedding conditioned on activity, motion, temperature, quality and history; detect deviations with calibrated uncertainty.

Evidence: context-matched residuals, fewer false alerts, future-time generalization.

PAPER 02 · MODEL METHODS

Multimodal SSL

Learn representations from synchronized PPG, accelerometer, gyroscope and temperature, emphasizing physiological content, contextual effects and personal adaptation.

Evidence: robust transfer and improved personalized downstream tasks versus strong SSL baselines.

PAPER 03 · CLINICAL TRANSLATION

Response Modeling

Model repeated within-person physiological trajectories after well-defined exposures or interventions using independent reference outcomes.

Evidence: prospective, participant-disjoint, clinically meaningful validation.

Unifying thesis: A useful personalized wearable model should distinguish expected context-dependent variation from unexpected physiological change—without treating every embedding deviation as a disease signal.

PAPER 1 · PUBLIC-DATA METHODS

CAPER: Context-Aware Personalized Embedding Residuals

Suggested title: Context-Conditioned Personalized Baseline Learning for Wearable PPG Change Detection

Research gap

  • Self-supervised wearable embeddings and person-level aggregation already exist.
  • Person-specific means do not describe how “normal” PPG varies across contexts.
  • Need evaluation of context-conditioned uncertainty and future-observation deviation.

Testable contributions

  • Estimate expected PPG embeddings conditional on person, activity, motion, temperature and history.
  • Use standardized residuals and uncertainty-aware change scores.
  • Evaluate false-alert reduction and held-out chronological generalization.

Core model

zₜ = fPPG(xₜ) ; (μᵤₜ, Σᵤₜ) = g(u, cₜ, hₜ)
rₜ = zₜ − μᵤₜ
Dₜ² = rₜᵀ (Σᵤₜ + λI)⁻¹ rₜ

Start with regularized diagonal covariance or shrinkage. A high Dₜ indicates deviation from a modeled baseline, not disease.

Key experiments

E1 · Baseline hierarchy

Population → person → person+activity → +motion → +temperature → +history. Compare held-out prediction error and calibration.

E2 · Change detection

Evaluate controlled state transitions and context-induced false alerts using AUROC, AUPRC and false alerts at fixed sensitivity.

E3 · Calibration budget

Vary the amount of initial personal data; quantify performance, context coverage and abstention on unseen contexts.

E4 · Transfer

Subject-disjoint training with initial adaptation on held-out participants; test on later contiguous observations.

Primary data: PPG-DaLiA (activity/motion), WESAD (stress/neutral), CATSA (cognitive-task perturbations). Important: these datasets are not substitutes for multiweek personal monitoring.

Acceptance criterion: consistent participant-level improvement over simple personal and activity-conditioned baselines, with uncertainty calibration and fewer context-induced false alarms.

PAPER 2 · REPRESENTATION METHODS

Personalization-Aware Multimodal Wearable SSL

Suggested title: Learning Context-Separable Physiological Representations from Multimodal Wearable Signals

Research gap

  • Generic PPG SSL and multimodal wearable foundation models are established directions.
  • Simple fusion may encode motion artifacts rather than physiological information.
  • The useful question is whether SSL can support both contextual explanation and sensitive within-person change detection.

Potential contributions (to validate)

  • Quality-aware, asynchronous multimodal pretraining.
  • Complementary physiology-sensitive and context-sensitive representation paths.
  • Objectives for context prediction, masked reconstruction and future latent prediction.
  • Personalization-aware transfer, not generic benchmark accuracy alone.

Baselines: PPG-only frozen encoder; TS2Vec; SimMTM; naive concatenation; late fusion; shared Transformer; feature-level fusion; context-only model. Include simple PPG morphology and pulse-rate variability features.

Key evaluations: activity recognition, signal-quality robustness, held-out participant transfer, missing-modality resilience, device/sample-rate shift, and Paper 1 baseline/change-detection performance.

Novelty caution: A new fusion Transformer plus standard SSL losses is not a sufficiently differentiated contribution. The paper needs a precise training objective or generalization property that strong multimodal baselines cannot achieve.
PAPER 3 · CLINICAL METHODS + VALIDATION

Personalized Physiological Response Modeling

Suggested title: Context-Adjusted Wearable Representation Trajectories for Within-Person Physiological Response Assessment

Research gap

  • Wearable physiological changes are indirect and nonspecific.
  • Person-level disease classification can obscure response dynamics.
  • Need to test whether context-adjusted trajectories add predictive value beyond standard clinical and wearable features.

Conditional contributions

  • Represent baseline → exposure → response → recovery trajectories.
  • Quantify subject-specific magnitude, duration and recovery.
  • Evaluate incremental clinical utility using independently measured outcomes.
Prospective response-study design
Repeated pre-event baselineStable recording context + validated reference measurements
↓
Predefined exposure / naturally observed eventClinically approved protocol; do not infer exposure from PPG alone
↓
Post-event response trajectoryDeviation magnitude · Time to peak · Area under trajectory · Recovery
↓
Independent reference outcomeValidated CGM / blood glucose, ABPM / cuff BP, ECG or other task-appropriate ground truth

Choose one clinical endpoint first. A glucose-associated response study requires validated glucose reference data and a suitable approved protocol; a blood-pressure study requires validated cuff/ABPM measurements. Do not use glucose-associated PPG deviations as evidence of glucose estimation without reference validation.

Baselines: demographics + reference clinical predictors; raw PPG/HR/PRV features; population embeddings; per-person raw embeddings; CAPER residuals; context-only predictors; time-series response model without personalization.

Metrics: task-specific error and calibration, participant-disjoint generalization, external validation, response timing, uncertainty, and incremental benefit beyond established predictors. Prespecify the endpoint and statistical plan.

Data dependency: Public stress/activity datasets cannot validate clinical disease detection. Paper 3 should begin only after ethics approval (where applicable), reference-measurement access, repeat observations and sufficient cohort design are established.

Required ablations

Remove activity, ACC, GYRO, temperature, history, uncertainty modeling and quality filtering one at a time. Compare frozen vs adapted encoders and vary personal calibration duration.

Leakage controls

Chronological non-overlapping test periods; subject-disjoint model selection; train-only normalization; no future-baseline updates; no oracle activity labels in deployed evaluation; participant-level confidence intervals.

Phase II · Paper 2 (indicatively months 7–14)

Build multimodal pretraining corpus → establish naive-fusion and SSL baselines → design and ablate personalization-aware objective → test subject/device transfer → manuscript. Go/no-go: the new objective must improve meaningful transfer or robustness outcomes, not just in-sample loss.

Phase III · Paper 3 (dependent on clinical partnership and data)

Choose one outcome → prospective protocol and approvals → repeated reference-labeled collection → locked evaluation → external validation → clinical-methods manuscript. Clinical data collection can be planned in parallel but should not be treated as already available.

GATE 1

Is personalization useful?

Compare CAPER with per-user/activity baselines on future periods.

GATE 2

Is new SSL necessary?

Beat strong pretrained, generic time-series and simple-fusion controls.

GATE 3

Is clinical signal real?

Demonstrate incremental utility against independent validated outcomes.

2026 novelty positioning and reading list

These works are essential comparators, not proof that CAPER's exact combination is unprecedented. Complete a systematic review before asserting a first-of-its-kind contribution.

  1. Google AI-PPG-ACC-HTN (2026) — self-supervised PPG/ACC representations and multi-day aggregation for hypertension screening. Closest high-priority comparison.
  2. NormWear (2026) — multimodal physiological wearable foundation modeling.
  3. SOTER (2026) — generative wearable physiological time-series foundation modeling.
  4. DNA-PPG (IJCAI 2026) — morphology-aware PPG self-supervised representations.
  5. SIGMA-PPG (ICML 2026) — PPG pretraining with prior-guided masking.
  6. Pulse-PPG and PaPaGei — candidate pretrained PPG encoders.
  7. TS2Vec and SimMTM — general time-series SSL baselines.
  8. GlucoFM (2026) — a distinct CGM-based foundation model; relevant to baseline/response concepts, not a PPG glucose estimator.
Scientific guardrail: PPG measures peripheral blood-volume dynamics, not glucose directly. Residual scores do not identify disease. A glucose response is not a diabetes diagnosis. Clinical claims require independent reference measurements and task-appropriate validation.
